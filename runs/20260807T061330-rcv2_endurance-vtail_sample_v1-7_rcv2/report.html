The two methods are sampled over the same alpha window and run through the same estimator, so the only difference between these numbers is the aerodynamics. They are not expected to agree in LEVEL — this sweep is inviscid and carries no viscous Cm — so the row that matters is the middle one. LL reporting a sign change that the VLM does not see places the nonlinearity in LL's viscous Cm or its discretization, and is not by itself a verdict on the aeroplane (FINDINGS §21).

One mechanism, not two: the station where the local closure angle first crosses the threshold defines an enlarged effective base, and unrecovered base pressure is charged there. The coefficient (CD_BASE) is declared and uncalibrated — matched to AeroSandbox's own low-Mach base-drag value so a future numeric cross-check compares like with like, not fitted to anything this project has measured. Angles use the d_eq-equivalent axisymmetric convention, so a 68 × 88 section's vertical-plane closure is ~14% steeper in tangent than reported here.

What limits this result

Not proven infeasible: trim did not converge at V = 8.0 m/s. These points are absent from the sweep because there is no operating point to report, not because none exists — the 2-D root-find ran out of progress from every start it was given. Read them as unknown.

The static margin changes sign inside its own window. The reported 0.0572 is a least-squares slope over an alpha window about trim, not a derivative at trim — and the local dCm/dCL goes negative at 6.23°, so on this model the aircraft is not positively stable there. Cm(alpha) nonlinearity is the known dominant fidelity limit of this estimator: read the margin as an average over the window, and do not set the CG from it alone.

Notes

  • Stall via wing-level CLmax knockdown; critical-section method arrives at M2/M3.
  • Smooth polars only; tripped-polar dual evaluation arrives at M2.
  • Propulsion chain is uncalibrated (no measurement path) — rankings over absolutes.
  • NOT PROVEN INFEASIBLE — trim did not converge at V = 8.0 m/s (the 2-D root-find ran out of progress from every start it was given). These points are excluded from the sweep because there is no operating point to report, not because none exists; read them as unknown rather than as a limit of the aeroplane.
  • STATIC MARGIN CHANGES SIGN INSIDE ITS OWN WINDOW: the reported 0.0572 is a least-squares slope over [-2.0, 0.0, 2.0] deg about trim, but the LOCAL dCm/dCL at alpha = 6.23 deg is -0.0153 — the aircraft is not positively stable there on this model. Cm(alpha) nonlinearity is the known dominant fidelity limit of this SM estimator (FINDINGS); treat the margin as an average, and do not fly the CG on the strength of it alone.
  • Static margin is reported OUT of range at the re-evaluation's own best speed (9.500 m/s), but the optimized design meets the window at the speed it was solved for (9.742 m/s, SM 0.08000). The sweep's airworthiness filter does not carry the SM window — see `constraints.sm_read_at`.
  • The static margin's sign change SURVIVES a 16-panel mesh (0.0572 → 0.0534), so refinement does not explain it. Combined with an inviscid VLM sweep that is monotone over the same window, the nonlinearity is the VISCOUS Cm at this Reynolds number — treat it as a property of the aeroplane.
  • CHAMPION STATIC MARGIN IS MESH-DEPENDENT: 0.0572 at the in-loop resolution against 0.0534 at 16 panels/section. The stability window is 0.07 wide and this moves the answer inside it.
  • STATIC-MARGIN SIGN FLIP IS NOT CONFIRMED BY AN INDEPENDENT METHOD: LiftingLine reports a local dCm/dCL going negative inside the window, but the VLM sweep over the same alphas is monotone and returns SM = 0.0530 against LL's 0.0572. The two methods differ by viscosity, so this does not settle which is right — it localizes the nonlinearity to LL's viscous Cm or its discretization, and the margin should not be treated as failing on LL's word alone.
  • M3 NLP: trimmed (explicit deflection), SM window, gust margin, spar stress/deflection sizing, ballast cap, battery-position balance.
