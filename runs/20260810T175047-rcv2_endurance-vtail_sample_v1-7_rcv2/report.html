The two methods are sampled over the same alpha window and run through the same estimator, so the only difference between these numbers is the aerodynamics. They are not expected to agree in LEVEL — this sweep is inviscid and carries no viscous Cm — so the row that matters is the middle one. LL reporting a sign change that the VLM does not see places the nonlinearity in LL's viscous Cm or its discretization, and is not by itself a verdict on the aeroplane (FINDINGS §21).

One mechanism, not two: the station where the local closure angle first crosses the threshold defines an enlarged effective base, and unrecovered base pressure is charged there. What limits this result

Not proven infeasible: trim did not converge at V = 8.0 m/s. These points are absent from the sweep because there is no operating point to report, not because none exists — the 2-D root-find ran out of progress from every start it was given. Read them as unknown.

The static margin changes sign inside its own window. The reported 0.0665 is a least-squares slope over an alpha window about trim, not a derivative at trim — and the local dCm/dCL goes negative at 6.18°, so on this model the aircraft is not positively stable there. Cm(alpha) nonlinearity is the known dominant fidelity limit of this estimator: read the margin as an average over the window, and do not set the CG from it alone.

Notes

  • DESIGN NOT TRUSTWORTHY FOR FLIGHT OR CONSTRUCTION: no airworthy operating point exists in the final sweep; reported operating point misses the static-margin window; static margin changes sign inside its evaluation window. The optimization result remains useful diagnostically, but its headline objective is not an airworthy design recommendation.
  • NO AIRWORTHY OPERATING POINT EXISTS IN THIS SWEEP. Every one of the 17 speeds that trimmed breaks at least one airworthiness rule, so there was nothing legal to choose from and what is reported is the least-bad ILLEGAL point: static margin 0.0665 against a 0.08 limit; minimum local static margin -0.009777 against a 0 limit. This is not a design. Read the objective as an upper bound the aeroplane cannot actually reach, and fix the aircraft — balance, control throw or wing loading — before reading anything else in this artifact.
  • Stall via wing-level CLmax knockdown; critical-section method arrives at M2/M3.
  • Smooth polars only; tripped-polar dual evaluation arrives at M2.
  • Propulsion chain is uncalibrated (no measurement path) — rankings over absolutes.
  • NOT PROVEN INFEASIBLE — trim did not converge at V = 8.0 m/s (the 2-D root-find ran out of progress from every start it was given). These points are excluded from the sweep because there is no operating point to report, not because none exists; read them as unknown rather than as a limit of the aeroplane.
  • STATIC MARGIN CHANGES SIGN INSIDE ITS OWN WINDOW: the reported 0.0665 is a least-squares slope over [-2.0, 0.0, 2.0] deg about trim, but the LOCAL dCm/dCL at alpha = 6.18 deg is -0.0098 — the aircraft is not positively stable there on this model. Cm(alpha) nonlinearity is the known dominant fidelity limit of this SM estimator (FINDINGS); treat the margin as an average, and do not fly the CG on the strength of it alone.
  • Static margin is OUT of range at the re-evaluation's reported speed (9.500 m/s), although the NLP met the window at 9.684 m/s (SM 0.08000). NO point in the sweep passed every airworthiness rule, so this is the least-bad ILLEGAL fallback, not an all-clear.
  • The static margin's sign change SURVIVES a 16-panel mesh (0.0665 → 0.0620), so refinement does not explain it. Combined with an inviscid VLM sweep that is monotone over the same window, the nonlinearity is the VISCOUS Cm at this Reynolds number — treat it as a property of the aeroplane.
  • CHAMPION STATIC MARGIN IS MESH-DEPENDENT: 0.0665 at the in-loop resolution against 0.0620 at 16 panels/section. The stability window is 0.07 wide and this moves the answer inside it.
  • THE FINENESS CEILING IS BINDING: the pod sits at f = 8.000000 against a declared ceiling of 8. That ceiling is a MODEL-validity bound — the Hoerner form factor keeps falling to f ~ 16 while the real minimum-drag band for a body of revolution is f ~ 6-7 — so the optimizer is being stopped by a declared number rather than by the aeroplane, and the slenderness it wants is not established to be real. Landing on it is a defect report against the fuselage drag model, not an optimum.
  • THE BOAT-TAIL FLOOR IS STILL ACTIVE: pod_tail sits at 1.800000 x d_eq against a floor of 1.8, with theta_max 18.9 deg against a 12 deg separation threshold. The floor is a PROXY for the afterbody physics and was kept for one battery to find out whether the new base-drag term had made it inactive. It has not: the term does not hold the tail cone open on its own, so the floor must NOT be deleted as a measured no-op and the correlation constants are what to revisit (docs/FUSELAGE_DRAG_PLAN.md 7).
  • M3 NLP: trimmed (explicit deflection), SM window, gust margin, spar stress/deflection sizing, ballast cap, battery-position balance.
  • Adaptive multistart: nominal and perturbed_0 converged to strict numerical consensus, so perturbed_1 was skipped as redundant. Exact tolerances and observed errors are recorded under performance.optimization.multistart_consensus.
